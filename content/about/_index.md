## About This Project

The Hofwerk project began with the purchase of an abandoned farmstead in Anhalt, Eastern Germany, in late 2020. The property — a three-sided complex of farmhouse, barn, workshop, and stables — dates from 1884, the Wilhelmine period, with later additions from the GDR era.

What started as a renovation has become a think tank and living experiment: how might we organize learning, work, and community life differently?

### The Research

The academic foundation is laid out in a peer-reviewed article published in *Frontiers in Sociology* (May 2023) by Joachim Broecher and Janet F. Painter: [Transformative community projects in East Germany's rural spaces](https://doi.org/10.3389/fsoc.2023.1164293).

Three documentation volumes — *Tomasz*, *Ludwik*, and *Ragnar* — chronicle the physical and intellectual development of the project through hundreds of photographs and reflections.

A fourth volume was published by De Gruyter in December 2025, anchoring the work in broader academic discourse on transformation, community, and education.

### Key Themes

- **Communitarianism** — social embedding without collectivist coercion
- **Unconditional Basic Income** — financial foundation for community participation
- **Self-Directed Learning** — alternatives to institutional schooling
- **Entrepreneurship** — community-based enterprise in structurally weak regions
- **Ecological Transformation** — sustainable building, farming, and living

### The People

The project is led by the Broecher family, with academic support from the University of Flensburg and international collaborators. The documentation volumes are named after character prototypes rooted in Polish-German educational exchange traditions.
